About the Project

A transit-focused redesign for movement, access, and everyday use.

This project reimagines Montefiore Square in Harlem as a more effective public space for people moving between the subway, buses, school, and nearby neighborhood destinations. The goal is to transform the plaza from a confusing and congested pass-through area into a clear multimodal hub that supports walking, waiting, and wayfinding.

The redesign follows the Transit Hub Harlem project brief by focusing on pedestrian flow, transit access, waiting areas, navigation, and the overall quality of everyday public use. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

The Problem

Why the current plaza does not work well as a transit space.

Existing Issues

Montefiore Square currently functions more like leftover space than a coordinated transit plaza. People moving between the 137th Street subway station, nearby bus stops, and surrounding sidewalks experience several problems:

  • Congestion near major pedestrian entry points
  • Unclear circulation between transit stops
  • Weak wayfinding for first-time users
  • Limited comfortable waiting space
  • Construction and street conditions that reduce clarity and safety

Essential Question

How can Montefiore Square be redesigned to better serve people moving between transit, school, shopping, and neighborhood destinations? :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

Project Goal

The design aims to reduce friction between transit modes while improving comfort, accessibility, and legibility. Instead of forcing pedestrians to improvise their routes, the plaza should guide movement clearly and support short-term waiting, sitting, and gathering.

Site and Context

Reading Montefiore Square as a movement space.

Existing site view showing the edge condition of Broadway, construction barriers, and a street environment that feels dominated by movement conflict rather than pedestrian clarity.

Movement Analysis

The plaza sits in a location where pedestrians are constantly transitioning: from subway to sidewalk, from sidewalk to bus stop, and from street edge to open plaza space. This creates overlapping paths rather than one clear circulation system.

During busy periods, people are likely to choose the shortest route instead of the safest or most comfortable one. That behavior signals that the current design does not communicate priority routes clearly.

Waiting Behavior

A transit plaza must do more than move people through. It must also support waiting. Right now, the site offers limited structured places to pause, sit, or orient yourself before continuing a trip.

This makes the space less useful for students, older adults, bus riders, and anyone who needs a moment to rest or confirm where to go next.

Design Options

Testing multiple directions before choosing a final proposal.

Option 1: The Green Shelter

This concept focuses on comfort. It adds shaded seating, planting, and a calmer plaza atmosphere that encourages people to stay longer rather than only pass through.

Strengths: better comfort, softer public space character, stronger neighborhood feel.

Weakness: it improves the waiting experience more than the movement system, so transit connections remain only partially resolved.

Option 2: Wayfinding Plaza

This concept prioritizes circulation. It introduces clearer pedestrian paths, better visual organization, stronger edges, and improved navigation between the subway, bus stops, and surrounding sidewalks.

Strengths: clearer movement, better transit legibility, less confusion.

Weakness: it risks feeling too movement-focused unless paired with seating and comfort features.

Design Decision

Both options contributed useful ideas. Option 1 addressed comfort and identity. Option 2 solved navigation and circulation more directly. The final proposal combines the strongest parts of both.

The Final Proposal

A balanced transit plaza for movement, access, and everyday use.

Final concept direction: a transit-oriented public plaza with clearer routes, structured waiting areas, and a more welcoming edge for everyday users.

Key Moves

  • Create clearer pedestrian desire lines between transit points
  • Add visible wayfinding elements at decision points
  • Introduce seating near, but not inside, primary movement routes
  • Use planting and paving to organize the space without blocking flow
  • Improve the relationship between waiting space and walking space

Why This Works

The final proposal treats the plaza as both a transit connector and a public place. It does not force a choice between speed and comfort. Instead, it separates fast movement routes from pause points, making the site easier to understand and more useful for everyday transit users.

This directly responds to the Transit Hub Harlem brief, which emphasizes movement analysis, pedestrian flow, transit access, wayfinding, and waiting strategies. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

Implementation Strategy

How the redesign could happen in realistic phases.

Phase 1

Quick improvements such as painted wayfinding, temporary seating, and clearer bus and subway connection signage.

Phase 2

Medium-term upgrades including durable seating, planting beds, improved paving, and better-defined waiting zones.

Phase 3

Long-term investment in a fully integrated plaza identity with cohesive materials, lighting, and permanent transit-oriented public-space improvements.

Reflection on AI

What AI helped with, and what still required human judgment.

AI was useful for quickly generating visual possibilities and helping compare different redesign directions. It made it easier to test how Montefiore Square might look with more greenery, better organization, or stronger wayfinding.

However, AI could not determine which proposal best matched the real movement problems of the site on its own. Human judgment was necessary to evaluate circulation, decide what felt realistic, and select the strongest combination of comfort and transit function.

This process aligns with the course requirement that AI should support research, visualization, writing, and design exploration, but not replace observation or critical thinking. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

Conclusion

Transit Hub Harlem proposes a Montefiore Square that is easier to navigate, more welcoming to use, and more responsive to the daily realities of movement in Harlem. By combining wayfinding, waiting space, and pedestrian clarity, the plaza can become a true neighborhood gateway instead of a confusing leftover space.
