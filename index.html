About the Project

A transit-focused redesign for movement, access, and everyday use.

This project reimagines Montefiore Square in Harlem as a more effective public space for people moving between the subway, buses, school, and nearby neighborhood destinations.

Located near the 137th Street–City College station, the goal is to transform the plaza into a clear multimodal hub that supports walking, waiting, and safer connections.

The Problem

Why the current plaza does not work well.

Issues

Heavy congestion near subway exits and bus stops.

Unclear pedestrian routes causing confusion.

Poor wayfinding for new users.

Limited seating and waiting space.

Goal

Improve safety, navigation, and comfort by organizing movement and creating better waiting areas.

Site

Existing conditions showing congestion and unclear movement paths.

Design Options

Green Shelter

Adds seating, shade, and greenery to improve comfort and create a more welcoming public space.

Wayfinding Plaza

Focuses on clearer pedestrian paths, signage, and organization of movement between transit points.

Final Proposal

Final design combining shade, seating, and clear pedestrian routes.

The final proposal combines the strengths of both design options, creating a space that supports both movement and waiting. Clear paths reduce confusion while seating and shade improve comfort for daily users.

Implementation

Phase 1 includes painted paths and temporary seating. Later phases add permanent seating, shade structures, and improved crossings to fully transform the plaza.

Reflection

AI helped generate visual ideas quickly, but human judgment was needed to choose the most realistic and effective solutions for the site.

Conclusion

This redesign improves safety, movement, and usability, transforming Montefiore Square into a clearer and more functional transit hub.
