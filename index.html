Transit Hub Harlem

Redesigning Montefiore Square as a safer, clearer, and more comfortable transit gateway.

About the Project

A transit-focused redesign for movement, access, and everyday use.

This project reimagines Montefiore Square in Harlem as a more effective public space for people moving between the subway, buses, school, and nearby neighborhood destinations. Instead of treating the plaza as leftover space, this proposal treats it as an important part of daily public life and neighborhood movement.

The goal is to transform the plaza from a confusing and congested pass-through area into a clear multimodal hub that supports walking, waiting, orientation, and safer connections between transit points. This directly follows the Transit Hub Harlem brief, which focuses on pedestrian flow, transit access, waiting areas, wayfinding, and movement between destinations. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

The Problem

Why the current plaza does not work well as a transit space.

Existing Issues

  • Congestion near major pedestrian entry points
  • Unclear circulation between transit stops
  • Weak wayfinding for first-time users
  • Limited comfortable waiting space
  • Unsafe and confusing crossings

Essential Question

How can Montefiore Square be redesigned to better serve people moving between transit, school, shopping, and neighborhood destinations?

Project Goal

The goal is to reduce congestion, improve navigation, and create a safer, more comfortable transit space for everyday users. A successful redesign should help people understand where to walk, where to wait, and how to move through the site without confusion or conflict.

Site and Context

Understanding Montefiore Square as a movement space.

Existing site conditions showing congestion, unclear pedestrian routes, and a street environment dominated by movement conflict rather than pedestrian clarity.

Movement Analysis

Montefiore Square sits in a location where pedestrians are constantly transitioning between the subway, nearby bus stops, sidewalks, and neighborhood destinations. Because of that, the space feels busy but not well organized. People are forced to improvise their routes instead of being guided clearly through the plaza.

This creates overlapping circulation patterns, especially near entry points and crossings. Instead of supporting smooth movement, the current layout increases confusion and makes the plaza harder to read for both frequent users and first-time visitors.

Waiting and Use Patterns

A transit plaza must do more than move people through. It also has to support waiting, pausing, and decision-making. Right now, the site offers very few clearly defined places where someone can sit, orient themselves, or wait comfortably for a bus.

This matters because transit users are not all moving at the same speed. Students, older adults, bus riders, and neighborhood pedestrians all use the space differently. A stronger design needs to separate fast movement paths from waiting space so the plaza can work better for everyone.

Design Options

Testing multiple directions before choosing a final proposal.

Option 1: Green Shelter

Option 1 focuses on comfort by adding shade, greenery, and more seating. This approach makes the plaza feel calmer, softer, and more welcoming for people who want to sit, wait, or spend time there.

Its biggest strength is atmosphere. It improves the experience of staying in the plaza and gives the site a stronger public-space identity. However, it does not fully solve the circulation and navigation issues that affect daily transit users.

As a result, this option is good at making the space more pleasant, but weaker at organizing movement between the subway, buses, and surrounding sidewalks.

Option 2: Wayfinding Plaza

Option 2 focuses on movement by creating clearer pedestrian paths and stronger wayfinding between subway exits, bus stops, and sidewalks. This makes the plaza easier to understand and helps reduce confusion at key decision points.

Its biggest strength is circulation. It creates a clearer logic for movement and responds more directly to the transit function of the site. However, it risks feeling too movement-focused unless it is balanced with more seating, shade, and comfort features.

This option is stronger as a transit strategy, but weaker as a public-space experience if used on its own.

Comparison and Design Decision

Both options contributed important ideas. Option 1 improved comfort, identity, and the feeling of staying in the plaza. Option 2 improved circulation, navigation, and the clarity of movement between transit points.

The final proposal combines the strongest parts of both. Instead of choosing between comfort and movement, the redesign treats both as necessary parts of a successful transit plaza.

Final Proposal

A balanced transit plaza for movement, comfort, and everyday use.

Final concept combining clearer movement routes with more comfortable public-space features.

Key Design Moves

  • Create clearer pedestrian routes between transit points
  • Add visible wayfinding at important decision points
  • Separate waiting areas from the fastest circulation paths
  • Introduce seating and shade to improve comfort
  • Make the plaza easier to understand for daily users and visitors

Why This Proposal Works

The final design combines clear pedestrian paths with comfortable seating areas, creating a space that supports both movement and waiting. It improves circulation without making the plaza feel harsh or purely functional.

This proposal takes the strongest parts of both earlier options. It improves navigation and reduces confusion while also making the plaza more welcoming for everyday users who need to sit, wait, or orient themselves before continuing their trip.

This redesign transforms Montefiore Square from a space people pass through into a space that supports movement, decision-making, and everyday public life. Implementation Strategy

How the redesign could happen in realistic phases.

Phase 1

Quick improvements such as painted wayfinding, temporary seating, and clearer bus and subway connection signage.

Phase 2

Medium-term upgrades including durable seating, planting beds, improved paving, and better-defined waiting zones.

Phase 3

Long-term investment in a fully integrated plaza identity with cohesive materials, lighting, and permanent transit-oriented public-space improvements.

Reflection on AI

What AI helped with and what still required human judgment.

AI helped generate design ideas quickly and made it easier to explore multiple directions for the site. It was especially useful for testing different visual possibilities and comparing how Montefiore Square could look with more comfort features, stronger wayfinding, or a clearer circulation system.

However, some AI-generated ideas were too generic or did not fully respond to the actual movement and access problems of the plaza. Human judgment was needed to decide which ideas were realistic, which ones matched the site, and which design elements would actually improve daily use.

This project shows that AI is most effective as a tool for exploration, visualization, and rapid comparison, while human decision-making remains essential for real-world design solutions. Conclusion

Transit Hub Harlem proposes a Montefiore Square that is easier to navigate, more welcoming to use, and more responsive to the daily realities of movement in Harlem. By combining wayfinding, waiting space, and pedestrian clarity, the plaza can become a true neighborhood gateway instead of a confusing pass-through zone.
