About the Project

A transit-focused redesign for movement, access, and everyday use.

This project reimagines Montefiore Square in Harlem as a more effective public space for people moving between the subway, buses, school, and nearby neighborhood destinations. The goal is to transform the plaza from a confusing and congested pass-through area into a clear multimodal hub that supports walking, waiting, and wayfinding.

The redesign focuses on improving pedestrian flow, transit access, waiting areas, and navigation. Instead of treating the plaza as leftover space, this proposal treats it as an important part of everyday public movement in Harlem.

The Problem

Why the current plaza does not work well as a transit space.

Existing Issues

  • Congestion near major pedestrian entry points
  • Unclear circulation between transit stops
  • Weak wayfinding for first-time users
  • Limited comfortable waiting space
  • Unsafe and confusing crossings

Project Goal

The goal is to reduce congestion, improve navigation, and create a safer, more comfortable transit space for everyday users.

Essential Question

How can Montefiore Square be redesigned to better serve people moving between transit, school, shopping, and neighborhood destinations?

Site and Context

Understanding Montefiore Square as a movement space.

Existing site conditions showing congestion and unclear pedestrian routes.

Montefiore Square sits in a location where people are constantly transitioning between the subway, nearby bus stops, sidewalks, and neighborhood destinations. This makes the space feel busy but not well organized.

The current layout does not clearly guide people where to walk, where to wait, or where to pause. As a result, movement patterns overlap and the space feels less safe and less welcoming than it could be.

Design Options

Testing multiple directions before choosing a final proposal.

Option 1: Green Shelter

Option 1 focuses on comfort by adding shade, greenery, and more seating. This makes the plaza feel calmer and more welcoming for people who want to sit, wait, or spend time there.

Its strength is comfort and atmosphere. Its weakness is that it does not fully solve the circulation and navigation issues that affect daily transit users.

Option 2: Wayfinding Plaza

Option 2 focuses on movement by creating clearer pedestrian paths and stronger wayfinding between subway exits, bus stops, and sidewalks.

Its strength is better circulation and easier navigation. Its weakness is that it can feel too movement-focused unless it is balanced with comfort features like seating and shade.

Final Proposal

A balanced transit plaza for movement and everyday use.

Final concept combining clearer routes with more comfortable public space features.

The final design combines clear pedestrian paths with comfortable seating areas, creating a space that supports both movement and waiting.

This proposal takes the strongest parts of both earlier options. It improves navigation and circulation while also making the plaza more welcoming for everyday users.

This redesign transforms Montefiore Square from a space people pass through into a space that supports movement, comfort, and everyday public life.

Reflection on AI

What AI helped with and what still required human judgment.

AI helped generate design ideas quickly and made it easier to compare multiple directions for the plaza.

However, human judgment was needed to choose the most realistic and effective solution. AI could suggest visual possibilities, but it could not fully understand which ideas best solved the movement and access problems at the site.

This project shows that AI is most effective as a tool for exploration, while human decision-making is essential for real-world design solutions.

Conclusion

This project improves safety, movement, and usability, transforming Montefiore Square into a functional transit hub.
