About the Project

A transit-focused redesign for movement, access, and everyday use.

This project reimagines Montefiore Square in Harlem as a more effective public space for people moving between the subway, buses, school, and nearby neighborhood destinations. The goal is to transform the plaza from a confusing and congested pass-through area into a clear multimodal hub that supports walking, waiting, and wayfinding.

The redesign focuses on improving pedestrian flow, transit access, waiting areas, and navigation.

The Problem

Why the current plaza does not work well as a transit space.

Existing Issues

  • Congestion near major pedestrian entry points
  • Unclear circulation between transit stops
  • Weak wayfinding for first-time users
  • Limited comfortable waiting space
  • Unsafe and confusing crossings

Project Goal

The goal is to reduce congestion, improve navigation, and create a safer, more comfortable transit space for everyday users.

Site and Context

Existing site conditions showing congestion and unclear pedestrian routes.

Design Options

Option 1: Green Shelter

Focuses on comfort with seating, shade, and greenery.

Option 2: Wayfinding Plaza

Focuses on clearer movement paths and better navigation.

Final Proposal

The final design combines clear pedestrian paths with comfortable seating areas, creating a space that supports both movement and waiting.

Reflection on AI

AI helped generate design ideas quickly, but human judgment was needed to choose the most realistic and effective solution.

Conclusion

This project improves safety, movement, and usability, transforming Montefiore Square into a functional transit hub.
